## Why version 2 exists

In version 1, text-only triage did as well as Due Process, and the corpus allowed a shortcut: every phishing page was on a Tailspin host, almost every false report was on a Northwind Bank host, and the text model is given the customer list. It could score well by judging who owns the host.

Version 2 is designed to remove that shortcut, and it says so openly: **the twin pairs test what cannot be known from the text.** That is a test written to expose a limit of reading reports, so it favors any approach that opens the page. It measures that limit. It is not a sample of real abuse reports, where a report's wording does carry information.

- **8 twin pairs.** Both pages of a pair get the identical report. One page is abuse and one is legitimate. Both are owned by one customer with a neutral name, on neutrally named hosts, with paths that are only numbers, and abuse pages are split evenly across the two hosts. Nothing in the input to the text model separates the two pages.
- **5 mixed cases** break the old pattern: phishing on Northwind Bank hosts with a flat report, and strong false reports against legitimate pages on Tailspin hosts.
- The text model's input did not change: report text, URL and customer list.

How to read this:

- For Due Process the action is the strongest one its level allows, so this is the worst an analyst could do. A real analyst approves each action and sees the evidence.
- Text-only triage: a model reads the URL and the report and decides. It gets the customer list. It has no defense against injection in its prompt.
- Fetch without browser: one HTTP GET through the same proxy, with the same inventory check and the same level rule as Due Process. Only the browser is taken away.
- Abuse missed means no Suspend or Warn on a case labeled as abuse.
- Fetch without browser never reads the report. Its injection count is a page it missed, not an injection that worked.

## What these numbers do not show

- **The twin results are close to fixed by the design, on both sides.** Text-only triage gets almost the same input for both pages of a pair, so it can hardly answer them differently, and a pair needs different answers. That is the point of the test, and says nothing about how good the model is at reading reports.
- **Due Process's pairs are its easy case.** The abuse pages use techniques its signals are built to see. None uses the two known misses, a URL built at run time and a login in an iframe, which stay in the totals as `phish-runtime-url` and `phish-iframe`.
- The twin pairs show a limit of reading reports, not how often real reports are like this.
- The text model gives no evidence for its decision. That difference does not show in these columns.
- Every case ran once, on simple pages. The corpus was extended after seeing the v1 results.
